Settings

The Settings section controls how the device presents itself over Bluetooth: whether it's discoverable, how many connections it accepts at once, and how much detail it logs.

Options

Each option below changes one part of how the device presents itself.

Advertising

Turns Bluetooth advertising on or off. While on, the device is visible to Pokémon GO and the companion app when they scan. Turn it off once everything you need is already paired, so it stops showing up to new scans. Existing connections aren't affected by turning it off. The choice stays in place when a device connects or disconnects. Any phone in Bluetooth range can pair with the device and control it while Advertising is on. Keep it off when you are not pairing.

Status light

Turns the device's light on or off. See Status light. Forget all pairings turns the light back on.

Max connections

The largest number of simultaneous Bluetooth connections the device accepts, from 1 to 4. Lower it to reserve the device for fewer accounts. Raise it back up to 4 to allow the full set. See Multiple connections.

Log level

How much detail the device records internally: Info, Debug, or Verbose, from least to most detailed. Tap Cycle log level to step through the options. Verbose is the most useful setting when you're trying to reproduce a problem to report.

Default: Debug

Game monitor

Sends alerts on your phone when Pokémon GO reports a problem. See Game monitor.

Default: Off

Status light

The light on the device blinks in a pattern that tells you its state. When two states apply, the one higher in the list wins. With the Status light switch off, the light stays dark.

Three long flashes after power-on

Meaning: The device restarted after a crash. The Crash report in the app has the details.

Fast blinking

Meaning: The device has no secrets. Import your secrets file with the app, see Set up a device with no secrets.

1 to 4 blinks, then a pause

Meaning: That many Pokémon GO phones are connected.

Slow blinking

Meaning: The device waits for a phone to connect.

Off

Meaning: The device is not advertising and no phone is connected.

Theme

The app follows your phone's light or dark setting by default. To choose another look, open the About tab and tap Theme. Each tap switches between your phone's setting, light, and dark.

Language

The app is available in English and French. It uses your phone's language when it is one of these two. On Android 13 or newer, open the About tab and tap Language to pick a language for this app only. Older phones follow the phone language.

Changes are saved automatically

Advertising, Status light, Max connections, and Log level are saved the moment you change them. They are still set the next time the device powers on. If the device is not advertising after a restart, press its button to turn Advertising back on. A press under 3 seconds turns Advertising on or off. Holding the button for 3 to 9 seconds allows a firmware update instead.

Game monitor

The Game monitor watches the notification that Pokémon GO shows while it is connected to the device. When that notification reports a problem, the app shows its own alert, so you do not have to open the game.

  • Pokémon GO lost the device.
  • The bag is full.
  • The Pokémon box is full.
  • You are out of Poké Balls.
  • A Pokéstop spin gave no items.

To turn it on, switch on Alert me about Pokémon GO in Settings, read the notice, and tap Continue. Your phone then asks you to allow notifications and to give the app notification access. Grant both. The monitor works on the phone alone and needs no Bluetooth connection to the device.

To turn it off, switch it off in Settings. You can also take away the app's access in your phone's notification access settings. Tap Notification access settings to open them. In your phone's settings you can mute one kind of alert at a time.

The monitor depends on the texts Pokémon GO shows. After a game update, alerts may stop until the app is updated too. If no alerts arrive, see No game alerts arrive.
