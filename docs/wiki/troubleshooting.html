Troubleshooting

Common connection and usage problems, and how to fix them.

Companion app can't find the device

  • Make sure the device is powered on and its LED is active.
  • Turn Bluetooth on for your phone, and grant the app's Bluetooth permission if it was denied earlier — the app shows a message under the Connect button when that's the problem.
  • Move closer. Bluetooth Low Energy range shrinks fast through walls and at distance.
  • Check Advertising is turned on. If it was switched off, the device won't show up in a scan at all.
  • If the device is already holding as many connections as its Max connections limit allows, it won't accept a new one. Disconnect something first, or raise the limit from another already-connected client.

Connecting fails with an error message

The companion app shows the underlying Bluetooth error text below the Connect button so it can be reported if needed. A few you might see, and what they usually mean:

Message containsLikely cause
Bluetooth adapter is off / Bluetooth is disabled Turn Bluetooth on for your phone and try again.
connection timed out The device didn't respond in time. Move closer, make sure it's powered on, and retry.
connection cancelled The connection attempt was interrupted, often by tapping Connect again mid-scan. Just retry.
device not migrated The device connected, but doesn't support the companion app protocol. Its firmware needs to be updated to a version that includes it.
bonding failed Pairing itself was rejected. Forget the device from your phone's Bluetooth settings and try connecting again from scratch.

Device not appearing in Pokémon GO's pairing scan

  • Confirm Advertising is on — this is checked from the companion app or Status card.
  • Make sure the device isn't already at its Max connections limit.
  • Turn Bluetooth off and back on for your phone, and restart the Pokémon GO app.
  • Some phones need Bluetooth scanning permission granted to Pokémon GO specifically — check its app permissions if the scan comes up empty.

Autospin or Autocatch isn't triggering

  • Open Device Profiles and confirm the SPIN or CATCH dot is lit for the profile that matches your connected Pokémon GO account.
  • Autospin pauses on its own if your item bag is full, and Autocatch pauses if your Pokémon box is full or you're out of Poké Balls. Clear space in-game and it resumes automatically.
  • If more than one account uses the same device, double check you're toggling the profile for the right connection slot, not a different one.

Settings reset after a restart

Advertising, Max connections, and Log level only persist across a power cycle if you tap Save to device in Settings after changing them. A change that's never saved reverts to its last saved value the next time the device restarts.

Frequent disconnects or slow responses

  • Keep the device within a stable Bluetooth range — physical distance and obstructions are the most common cause.
  • A Log level of Verbose adds overhead. Switch it back to Info or Debug once you're done diagnosing an issue.
  • If several devices are connected at once, an already-busy connection slot can make everything feel slower. Check Client states to see what's connected.

Starting over from scratch

If a device is stuck in a bad state that the steps above don't fix, Reset session secrets in the Danger Zone clears its pairing identity entirely. You'll need to pair it again in both the companion app and Pokémon GO afterward, so use it as a last resort.
