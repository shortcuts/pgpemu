Troubleshooting

Common connection and usage problems, and how to fix them.

Companion app can't find the device

  • Make sure the device is powered on and its light is blinking. What the light patterns mean.
  • Turn Bluetooth on for your phone, and grant the app's Bluetooth permission if it was denied earlier — the app shows a message under the scan button when that's the problem, with a Turn on Bluetooth button or, after you chose "Don't ask again", an Open app settings button.
  • Tap Scan again — the list only fills while a scan is running.
  • Move closer. Bluetooth Low Energy range shrinks fast through walls and at distance.
  • Check Advertising is turned on. If it was switched off, the device won't show up in a scan at all.
  • If the device is already holding as many connections as its Max connections limit allows, it won't accept a new one. Disconnect something first, or raise the limit from another already-connected client.

Connecting fails with an error message

The companion app shows the underlying Bluetooth error text below the Connect button so it can be reported if needed. A few you might see, and what they usually mean:

Bluetooth adapter is off / Bluetooth is disabled

Turn Bluetooth on for your phone and try again.

connection timed out

The device didn't respond in time. Move closer, make sure it's powered on, and retry.

connection cancelled

The connection attempt was interrupted, often by tapping a device in the list twice. Just retry.

device not migrated

The device connected, but doesn't support the companion app protocol. Its firmware needs to be updated to a version that includes it.

bonding failed

Pairing itself was rejected. Forget the device from your phone's Bluetooth settings and try connecting again from scratch.

stale pairing could not be removed

The device lost its pairing, for example after a reflash, and the app could not remove the old one. Forget the device in your phone's Bluetooth settings, then connect again.

Device not appearing in Pokémon GO's pairing scan

  • Confirm Advertising is on — this is checked from the companion app or Status card.
  • Make sure the device isn't already at its Max connections limit.
  • Turn Bluetooth off and back on for your phone, and restart the Pokémon GO app.
  • Some phones need Bluetooth scanning permission granted to Pokémon GO specifically — check its app permissions if the scan comes up empty.

Autospin or Autocatch isn't triggering

  • Open Device Profiles and confirm the SPIN or CATCH dot is lit for the profile that matches your connected Pokémon GO account.
  • If your item bag is full, your Pokémon box is full, or you are out of Poké Balls, the device does not press the button for that action. Clear space or restock in the game. The device does not pause or resume on its own.
  • Each account keeps its own setting. If you turned Autospin or Autocatch off for an account, it stays off when that account reconnects. Tap the dot to turn it back on. With CATCH new, the device presses only for Pokémon you have not caught yet, so it skips the others.
  • If more than one account uses the same device, double check you're toggling the profile for the right connection slot, not a different one.

Frequent disconnects or slow responses

  • Keep the device within a stable Bluetooth range — physical distance and obstructions are the most common cause.
  • A Log level of Verbose adds overhead. Switch it back to Debug or Info once you're done diagnosing an issue.
  • If several devices are connected at once, an already-busy connection slot can make everything feel slower. Check Client states to see what's connected.

The device restarts on its own

Open the app and look for the Crash report card. Tap Share report and add it to a bug report. A weak power supply can also restart the device: try another cable or charger.

The app stopped reconnecting

After a dropped connection, the app tries again for about two minutes. If the device stays out of range or switched off for longer, the app gives up. Tap Scan and pick the device.

No game alerts arrive

Check these in order:

  1. The Alert me about Pokémon GO switch in Settings is on.
  2. The app has notification access. Tap Notification access settings and switch the app on.
  3. The app may show notifications. In your phone's app settings, allow notifications for PGPemu.
  4. Pokémon GO shows its own notification while it is connected to the device. Allow it in your phone's settings.

After a Pokémon GO update, alerts can stop until the app is updated too.

The app says to update the device firmware

The device runs firmware older than the app supports. Reflash it over USB with the latest release, then connect the app again.

The app says to hold the board button

The device only accepts a firmware update after you allow it on the device itself. Hold the button on the board for 3 seconds, let go, then start the update again within 2 minutes. Holding it for 10 seconds or more forgets all pairings instead. Pokémon GO must not be connected to the device during an update.

Starting over from scratch

If a device is stuck in a bad state that the steps above don't fix, tap Forget all pairings in the Danger Zone. It erases every pairing and setting but keeps the device's identity. No phone handy? Hold the board button for 10 seconds while the device is on, and it does the same. Afterwards, remove the device from your phone's Bluetooth settings if it still shows up, then pair again.

As a last resort, Reset session secrets erases the device's identity too. The device stops working until you import your secrets file again from the app, as described in Set up a device with no secrets.

Still stuck?

Refresh the dumps in Diagnostics, tap Share report, and paste the result into a bug report.
