Hardware

This page shows what to buy and how to wire it. Once the board is ready, put the software on it with Install & Update Firmware.

Board

Use an ESP32-C3 Super Mini. It is the only board tested so far. Other ESP32-C3 boards may work, but the light and button pins can differ.

Light and button

The Super Mini already has what you need. You wire nothing for basic use.

  • The status light is on pin 8. See Status light for what each pattern means.
  • The button is on pin 9. It is the board's built-in BOOT button.

To add your own light or button, wire them like this:

  1. Light: connect pin 8 to a small resistor (about 330 ohms), then to the long leg of an LED. Connect the short leg to GND.
  2. Button: connect one side to pin 9 and the other side to GND. The board holds the pin high by itself, so you add no resistor.

Holding the button for 10 seconds forgets all pairings. See Troubleshooting.

Power

The device draws about 0.05 A. Power it from a power bank, a USB charger, or a battery pack through the USB-C port.

  • Some power banks switch off when the draw is this low. Pick one with a low-current mode, or use a battery pack.
  • When the supply voltage drops too low, the board restarts. After such a restart, the device waits 60 seconds before it starts again. This stops it from restarting over and over on a weak battery. Charge the battery if you see this.

Case

A snap-fit case for the Super Mini is on MakerWorld. It has five options. Pick one that leaves the USB-C port and the button reachable.
